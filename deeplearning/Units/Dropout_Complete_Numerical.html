Dropout — Complete Numerical Problem

Problem Statement

A hidden layer contains 5 neurons with outputs:
a = [2, 4, 6, 8, 10]
During training, use a dropout probability p = 0.20. Calculate:
  1. Keep probability
  2. Inverse probability scaling factor
  3. A possible dropout mask
  4. The output after dropout and inverted scaling
  5. The mean activation before and after dropout
  6. The expected activation and explain why inverted dropout preserves the expected value

Step 1: Choose Dropout Probability

p = 0.20

Therefore, 20% of neurons are expected to be dropped during a training iteration.

Step 2: Calculate Keep Probability

q = 1 − p
q = 1 − 0.20 = 0.80

Thus, the probability that a neuron is retained is 80%.

Step 3: Calculate Inverse Probability Scaling

Inverse scaling factor = 1/q
1/0.80 = 1.25

Every retained activation is multiplied by 1.25 during training.

Step 4: Apply a Possible Dropout Mask

One possible random dropout mask is:

m = [1, 0, 1, 1, 0]

Here, neurons 2 and 5 are dropped in this particular training iteration.

Step 6: Mean Activation Before and After Dropout

Before dropout:

Mean = (2+4+6+8+10)/5 = 6

For the particular mask above, after inverted dropout:

Mean = (2.5+0+7.5+10+0)/5 = 4

The mean for one particular random mask does not have to equal the original mean.

Step 7: Expected Activation

For one neuron with activation a, the expected output after inverted dropout is:

E[a′] = q(a/q) + p(0)
E[a′] = a

For example, for the neuron whose original activation is 8:

E[a′] = 0.80(8/0.80) + 0.20(0)
E[a′] = 8
Expected activation is preserved by inverted dropout.
Physical meaning: Dropout deliberately removes a random subset of neurons during training so that the network does not depend too strongly on particular neurons. Inverted scaling compensates for the removed activations so that the expected activation magnitude remains approximately unchanged.

Step 8: During Testing / Inference

Dropout is switched OFF.

Training
Random neurons dropped
+ scaling
→
Testing
All neurons active
No dropout

Because inverted dropout scaling was already applied during training, no additional scaling is required during inference.
