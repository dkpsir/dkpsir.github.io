Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) — Numerical Problem

Problem

An email classifier predicts the probability that each email is Spam (1). Calculate the Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) for the 5 samples and determine the mean BCE loss.
EmailActual yPredicted Probability pComponent Loss
E110.90-log(0.90) = 0.1054
E200.20-log(0.20) = 0.2231
E310.60-log(0.60) = 0.5108
E400.05-log(0.05) = 0.0513
E510.30-log(0.30) = 1.2040
Total2.0946

Step 1: Binary Cross Entropy Formula

L = −[y log(p) + (1−y) log(1−p)]

For each sample, only the probability assigned to the actual class contributes to the loss.

Step 2: Calculate Individual Losses

E1: y=1, p=0.90

L₁ = −log(0.90) = 0.1053

E2: y=0, p=0.20

L₂ = −log(0.80) = 0.2231

E3: y=1, p=0.60

L₃ = −log(0.60) = 0.5108

E4: y=0, p=0.05

L₄ = −log(0.95) = 0.0513

E5: y=1, p=0.30

L₅ = −log(0.30) = 1.2040

Step 3: Total and Mean BCE

Total BCE = 0.1053 + 0.2231 + 0.5108 + 0.0513 + 1.2040 = 2.0945
Mean BCE = 2.0945 / 5 = 0.4189
Final Mean BCE Loss = 0.4189
Physical meaning of the final loss:
The mean BCE of 0.4189 represents the model's average penalty for the probability predictions made for these 5 emails. A value closer to 0 indicates predictions that are more confident and correct; larger values indicate poorer probability predictions, especially when the model is confidently wrong.
