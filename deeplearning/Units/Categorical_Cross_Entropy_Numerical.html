Categorical Cross Entropy (CCE) — Numerical Problem

Problem

A classification model predicts one of three mutually exclusive classes: Cat, Dog, Horse. For the 4 samples below, calculate the Categorical Cross Entropy (CCE) loss.
SampleActual ClassPredicted Probabilities
(Cat, Dog, Horse)		Individual Loss
S1Cat(0.70, 0.20, 0.10))0.3567
S2Dog(0.10, 0.80, 0.10)0.2231
S3Horse(0.20, 0.30, 0.50)0.6931
S4Cat(0.60, 0.25, 0.15)0.5108

Step 1: Categorical Cross Entropy Formula

Lᵢ = −Σ yᵢⱼ log(pᵢⱼ)

With one-hot encoded labels, only the probability assigned to the correct class contributes to the loss.

Lᵢ = −log(pcorrect)

Step 2: Calculate Individual Losses

S1 (Cat): L₁ = −log(0.70) = 0.3567
S2 (Dog): L₂ = −log(0.80) = 0.2231
S3 (Horse): L₃ = −log(0.50) = 0.6931
S4 (Cat): L₄ = −log(0.60) = 0.5108

Step 3: Calculate Mean CCE

Total Loss = 0.3567 + 0.2231 + 0.6931 + 0.5108 = 1.7838
Mean CCE = 1.7838 / 4 = 0.4459
Final Mean CCE Loss = 0.4459
Physical meaning of the final loss:
The mean CCE of 0.4459 represents the average penalty assigned to the model's predicted class probabilities for these 4 samples. A value closer to 0 means the model assigns high probability to the correct classes; a larger value means the model is assigning lower probability to the correct class, with a very large penalty when it is confidently wrong.
