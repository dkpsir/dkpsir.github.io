Short Answer Questions – 2 Marks +
A column is a vertical structural member subjected to axial compressive loads, typically fixed at one or both ends (e.g., pillars of a building). A strut is a structural member subjected to axial compressive loads that can be oriented in any direction (vertical, horizontal, or inclined) and is generally pin-jointed or hinged at the ends (e.g., members of a roof truss).
The four standard end conditions for a column are:
1. Both ends hinged (or pinned).
2. Both ends fixed.
3. One end fixed and the other end hinged.
4. One end fixed and the other end free.
Circumferential (or Hoop) stress: The tensile stress acting tangentially to the circumference of a cylinder subjected to internal pressure. It resists the bursting of the cylinder.

Longitudinal stress: The tensile stress acting parallel to the longitudinal axis of the cylinder. It resists the tearing of the cylinder across its cross-section.
1. The material of the cylinder is homogeneous and isotropic.
2. Plane cross-sections perpendicular to the longitudinal axis remain plane after the application of internal pressure.
3. The material is stressed within the elastic limit (Hooke's Law holds good).
Given: Pressure \(p = 2\ \text{N/mm}^2\), Diameter \(d = 25\ \text{mm}\), Thickness \(t = 2.5\ \text{mm}\).
\[\sigma_l = \frac{pd}{4t}\]
\[\sigma_l = \frac{2 \times 25}{4 \times 2.5} = \frac{50}{10} = 5\ \text{N/mm}^2\]
The longitudinal stress is 5 N/mm².
Radial stress varies logarithmically (often approximated as parabolically) across the thickness of the cylinder wall. It is maximum and compressive at the inner surface (equal to the internal fluid pressure) and minimum (usually zero) at the outer surface.
The slenderness ratio is defined as the ratio of the effective length (\(L_e\)) of a column to its least radius of gyration (\(k_{min}\)). It is a dimensionless quantity used to determine whether a column will fail by crushing or buckling.
\[\lambda = \frac{L_e}{k_{min}}\]
Essay / Long Answer Questions +
(a) Assumptions in Euler’s theory:
  • The column is initially perfectly straight, and the applied load is purely axial.
  • The cross-section of the column is uniform throughout its length.
  • The material of the column is perfectly elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic.
  • The length of the column is very large compared to its lateral dimensions.
  • The direct stress is very small compared with the bending stress.
  • The column fails solely by buckling, and yielding of the material is ignored.

(b) End conditions in columns:

The load-carrying capacity of a column heavily depends on how its ends are restrained. The "effective length" (\(L_e\)) represents the distance between two adjacent points of zero bending moment (points of inflection).

  • Both Ends Hinged: The column can rotate freely at both ends. \(L_e = l\)
  • Both Ends Fixed: The column is rigidly held in position and direction at both ends. \(L_e = \frac{l}{2}\)
  • One End Fixed, Other End Hinged: One end is rigid, while the other can rotate. \(L_e = \frac{l}{\sqrt{2}}\)
  • One End Fixed, Other End Free: One end is rigid, and the other is completely free to move laterally (like a flagpole). \(L_e = 2l\)
Given:
\[ \begin{aligned} l &= 6000\ \text{mm}\\ d &= 40\ \text{mm}, \quad t = 5\ \text{mm}\\ FOS &= 3\\ E &= 2 \times 10^5\ \text{N/mm}^2 \end{aligned} \]
External Diameter, \(D\)
\[D = d + 2t = 40 + 2(5) = 50\ \text{mm}\]
Moment of Inertia, \(I\)
\[I = \frac{\pi}{64} (D^4 - d^4) = \frac{\pi}{64} (50^4 - 40^4) = 181,132.61\ \text{mm}^4\]
Effective Length, \(L_e\) (Both ends hinged)
\[L_e = l = 6000\ \text{mm}\]
Crippling Load, \(P_e\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_e &= \frac{\pi^2 E I}{L_e^2}\\ &= \frac{\pi^2 (2 \times 10^5) (181,132.61)}{6000^2}\\ &= 9,931.78\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Crippling Load = 9.93 kN
Safe Load, \(P_s\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_s &= \frac{P_e}{FOS}\\ &= \frac{9,931.78}{3}\\ &= 3,310.59\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Safe Load = 3.31 kN
Given:
\[ \begin{aligned} b &= 150\ \text{mm} \\ d &= 200\ \text{mm} \\ l &= 6000\ \text{mm}\\ E &= 17,500\ \text{N/mm}^2\\ FOS &= 3 \end{aligned} \]
Moments of Inertia (\(I_{xx}\) and \(I_{yy}\))
\[I_{xx} = \frac{b \cdot d^3}{12} = \frac{150 \times 200^3}{12} = 100,000,000\ \text{mm}^4\]
\[I_{yy} = \frac{d \cdot b^3}{12} = \frac{200 \times 150^3}{12} = 56,250,000\ \text{mm}^4\]
Least Moment of Inertia, \(I_{min}\)
\[I_{min} = I_{yy} = 56,250,000\ \text{mm}^4\]
Effective Length, \(L_e\) (Both ends fixed)
\[L_e = \frac{l}{2} = \frac{6000}{2} = 3000\ \text{mm}\]
Crippling Load, \(P_e\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_e &= \frac{\pi^2 E I_{min}}{L_e^2}\\ &= \frac{\pi^2 (17,500) (56,250,000)}{3000^2}\\ &= 1,079,488\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Crippling Load = 1079.49 kN
Safe Load, \(P_s\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_s &= \frac{P_e}{FOS}\\ &= \frac{1,079,488}{3}\\ &= 359,829\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Safe Load = 359.83 kN
Given:
\[ \begin{aligned} l &= 1500\ \text{mm}, \quad d = 50\ \text{mm}\\ \sigma_c &= 560\ \text{N/mm}^2, \quad a = \frac{1}{1600}\\ E &= 1.2 \times 10^5\ \text{N/mm}^2, \quad FOS = 3 \end{aligned} \]
Area (\(A\))
\[A = \frac{\pi}{4}(50)^2 = 1963.5\ \text{mm}^2\]
Moment of Inertia (\(I\))
\[I = \frac{\pi}{64}(50)^4 = 306,796.16\ \text{mm}^4\]
Radius of Gyration (\(k\))
\[k = \sqrt{\frac{I}{A}} = \sqrt{\frac{306,796.16}{1963.5}} = 12.5\ \text{mm}\]
Effective Length, \(L_e\) (One fixed, one free)
\[L_e = 2l = 3000\ \text{mm}\]
Rankine's Formula:
Crippling Load, \(P_R\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_R &= \frac{\sigma_c A}{1 + a (\frac{L_e}{k})^2}\\ &= \frac{560 \times 1963.5}{1 + \frac{1}{1600} (\frac{3000}{12.5})^2}\\ &= 29,717.77\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Safe Load, \(P_{s(R)}\)
\[P_{s(R)} = \frac{29,717.77}{3} = 9,905.92\ \text{N}\]
Rankine Safe Load = 9.91 kN
Euler's Formula:
Crippling Load, \(P_E\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_E &= \frac{\pi^2 E I}{L_e^2}\\ &= \frac{\pi^2 (1.2 \times 10^5) (306,796.16)}{3000^2}\\ &= 40,372.76\ \text{N} \end{aligned} \]
Safe Load, \(P_{s(E)}\)
\[P_{s(E)} = \frac{40,372.76}{3} = 13,457.59\ \text{N}\]
Euler Safe Load = 13.46 kN
Given:
\[ \begin{aligned} l &= 4000\ \text{mm}\\ w &= 30\ \text{N/mm}\\ \delta &= 15\ \text{mm} \end{aligned} \]
Flexural Rigidity, \(EI\)
\[ \begin{aligned} \delta &= \frac{5 w l^4}{384 EI}\\ EI &= \frac{5 \times 30 \times (4000)^4}{384 \times 15}\\ EI &= 6.667 \times 10^{12}\ \text{N}\cdot\text{mm}^2 \end{aligned} \]
Case (i): One end fixed, other hinged
Effective Length, \(L_e\)
\[L_e = \frac{l}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4000}{\sqrt{2}} = 2828.43\ \text{mm}\]
Crippling Load, \(P_1\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_1 &= \frac{\pi^2 EI}{L_e^2}\\ &= \frac{\pi^2 (6.667 \times 10^{12})}{(2828.43)^2} \end{aligned} \]
\(P_1\) = 8224.67 kN
Case (ii): Both ends pinned
Effective Length, \(L_e\)
\[L_e = l = 4000\ \text{mm}\]
Crippling Load, \(P_2\)
\[ \begin{aligned} P_2 &= \frac{\pi^2 EI}{L_e^2}\\ &= \frac{\pi^2 (6.667 \times 10^{12})}{4000^2} \end{aligned} \]
\(P_2\) = 4112.33 kN
