UNIT 5 – THIN AND THICK CYLINDERS (IMPORTANT QUESTIONS)
PDF document with questions and answers: Updated Shortly
Essay / Long Answer Questions +

Lamé’s equations give the radial pressure and circumferential (hoop) stress at any radius of a thick cylinder.

Let \(r_1\) = internal radius, \(r_2\) = external radius, \(L\) = cylinder length, \(x\) = radius of an elementary ring, \(dx\) = its thickness, \(p_x\) = radial compressive pressure at radius \(x\), and \(\sigma_x\) = circumferential tensile stress at radius \(x\).

Figure 1: Thick cylinder and elementary ring
Derivation

Consider a ring of thickness \(dx\) and length \(L\). The inner radial pressure is \(p_x\); the outer radial pressure is \(p_x+dp_x\).

1. Bursting force
\[\begin{aligned} F_b &= 2p_x xL-2(p_x+dp_x)(x+dx)L\\ &=2L[p_xx-p_xx-p_xdx-x\,dp_x-dp_x\,dx]\\ &=-2L(p_xdx+x\,dp_x) \qquad \text{(i)} \end{aligned}\]

Here the second-order product \(dp_x\,dx\) is neglected.

2. Resisting force
\[F_r=\sigma_x(2L\,dx)\qquad\text{(ii)}\]

Equating the resisting and bursting forces:

\[\begin{aligned} 2\sigma_x L\,dx&=-2L(p_xdx+x\,dp_x)\\ \sigma_x&=-p_x-x\frac{dp_x}{dx}\qquad\text{(iii)} \end{aligned}\]
3. Longitudinal strain relation

For a long, uniform cylinder away from end restraints, longitudinal strain is constant. Let \(\sigma_l\) be the constant longitudinal stress, \(E\) Young’s modulus, and \(\mu\) Poisson’s ratio.

\[\begin{aligned} \epsilon_l&=\frac{\sigma_l}{E}-\frac{\mu\sigma_x}{E}+\frac{\mu p_x}{E}\\ \sigma_x-p_x&=\text{constant}=2a\\ \sigma_x&=p_x+2a\qquad\text{(iv)} \end{aligned}\]
4. Integration

Substituting (iv) into (iii):

\[\begin{aligned} p_x+2a&=-p_x-x\frac{dp_x}{dx}\\ x\frac{dp_x}{dx}&=-2(p_x+a)\\ \frac{dp_x}{p_x+a}&=-2\frac{dx}{x}\\ \ln|p_x+a|&=-2\ln x+C\\ p_x+a&=\frac{b}{x^2} \end{aligned}\]

Therefore the Lamé equations, using radial pressure positive in compression, are:

\[\boxed{p_x=\frac{b}{x^2}-a}\qquad\text{(18.1)}\] \[\boxed{\sigma_x=\frac{b}{x^2}+a}\qquad\text{(18.2)}\]

Here \(a\) and \(b\) are constants obtained from boundary conditions. For internal pressure \(p_0\) and zero external gauge pressure:

\[\begin{aligned} x=r_1&:\quad p_x=p_0\\ x=r_2&:\quad p_x=0 \end{aligned}\]

Sign convention: \(p_x\) denotes compressive radial pressure, so the signed radial stress is \(\sigma_r=-p_x\). Hoop tension \(\sigma_x\) is positive.

Given Data:
\[ \begin{aligned} d &= 120\ \text{cm} = 1200\ \text{mm}\\ t &= 1.5\ \text{cm} = 15\ \text{mm}\\ L &= 6\ \text{m} = 6000\ \text{mm}\\ p &= 2.5\ \text{N/mm}^2\\ E &= 2 \times 10^5\ \text{N/mm}^2\\ \mu &= 0.3 \end{aligned} \]
1. Calculate Stresses

Hoop Stress (\(\sigma_h\)):

\[\sigma_h = \frac{p d}{2t} = \frac{2.5 \times 1200}{2 \times 15} = 100\ \text{N/mm}^2\]

Longitudinal Stress (\(\sigma_l\)):

\[\sigma_l = \frac{p d}{4t} = \frac{2.5 \times 1200}{4 \times 15} = 50\ \text{N/mm}^2\]
2. Calculate Strains

Hoop Strain (\(\epsilon_h\)):

\[\epsilon_h = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_h - \mu \sigma_l) = \frac{100 - 0.3(50)}{2 \times 10^5} = 0.000425\]

Longitudinal Strain (\(\epsilon_l\)):

\[\epsilon_l = \frac{1}{E} (\sigma_l - \mu \sigma_h) = \frac{50 - 0.3(100)}{2 \times 10^5} = 0.0001\]

Volumetric Strain (\(\epsilon_v\)):

\[\epsilon_v = 2\epsilon_h + \epsilon_l = 2(0.000425) + 0.0001 = 0.00095\]
3. Calculate Physical Changes

Change in Diameter (\(\delta d\)):

\[\delta d = \epsilon_h \times d = 0.000425 \times 1200\]
\(\delta d\) = 0.51 mm

Change in Length (\(\delta L\)):

\[\delta L = \epsilon_l \times L = 0.0001 \times 6000\]
\(\delta L\) = 0.6 mm

Change in Volume (\(\delta V\)):

\[ V = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 L = \frac{\pi}{4} (1200)^2 (6000) = 6.7858 \times 10^9\ \text{mm}^3 \]
\[\delta V = \epsilon_v \times V = 0.00095 \times 6.7858 \times 10^9\]
\(\delta V\) = 6,446,548.13 mm³
Given Data:
\[\begin{aligned} d_1&=125\ \mathrm{mm},&r_1&=62.5\ \mathrm{mm}\\ d_2&=250\ \mathrm{mm},&r_2&=125\ \mathrm{mm}\\ p_0&=50\ \mathrm{MPa},&p_{\mathrm{external}}&=0 \end{aligned}\]
1. Apply Lamé’s equations
\[\begin{aligned} p_x&=\frac{b}{x^2}-a\\ \sigma_x&=\frac{b}{x^2}+a \end{aligned}\]

At the inner surface \(x=r_1=62.5\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(p_x=p_0=50\ \mathrm{MPa}\). At the outer surface \(x=r_2=125\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(p_x=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} 50&=\frac{b}{(62.5)^2}-a \qquad\text{(1)}\\ 0&=\frac{b}{(125)^2}-a \qquad\text{(2)} \end{aligned}\]
2. Determine constants \(a\) and \(b\)
\[\begin{aligned} a&=\frac{b}{15625}\\ 50&=\frac{b}{3906.25}-\frac{b}{15625}\\ &=\frac{3b}{15625}\\ b&=\frac{50(15625)}{3}=260416.67\ \mathrm{MPa\,mm^2}\\ a&=\frac{260416.67}{15625}=16.6667\ \mathrm{MPa} \end{aligned}\]

Hence, with \(x\) measured in millimetres:

\[\begin{aligned} p_x&=\frac{260416.67}{x^2}-16.6667\ \mathrm{MPa}\\ \sigma_x&=\frac{260416.67}{x^2}+16.6667\ \mathrm{MPa} \end{aligned}\]
3. Maximum and minimum circumferential stresses

The hoop stress decreases with increasing radius; therefore the maximum occurs at the inner surface and the minimum at the outer surface.

\[\begin{aligned} \sigma_{x,\max}&=\frac{260416.67}{(62.5)^2}+16.6667\\ &=83.33\ \mathrm{MPa}\quad\text{(tensile)}\\[5pt] \sigma_{x,\min}&=\frac{260416.67}{(125)^2}+16.6667\\ &=33.33\ \mathrm{MPa}\quad\text{(tensile)} \end{aligned}\]
Maximum hoop stress: \(\boxed{83.33\ \mathrm{MPa}}\) (inner surface)
Minimum hoop stress: \(\boxed{33.33\ \mathrm{MPa}}\) (outer surface)
4. Stress distribution sketch
Given Data:
\[ \begin{aligned} p &= 2\ \text{MPa} = 2\ \text{N/mm}^2\\ d &= 1\ \text{m} = 1000\ \text{mm}\\ t &= 10\ \text{mm} \end{aligned} \]

Check for thin cylinder: \(t/d = 10/1000 = 1/100 \le 1/20\). Membrane theory applies.

1. Hoop Stress (\(\sigma_h\))
\[\sigma_h = \frac{p \cdot d}{2t}\]
\[\sigma_h = \frac{2 \times 1000}{2 \times 10}\]
\(\sigma_h\) = 100 MPa (Tensile)
2. Longitudinal Stress (\(\sigma_l\))
\[\sigma_l = \frac{p \cdot d}{4t} = \frac{\sigma_h}{2}\]
\[\sigma_l = \frac{2 \times 1000}{4 \times 10}\]
\(\sigma_l\) = 50 MPa (Tensile)
