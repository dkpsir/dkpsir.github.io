Beam Deflections — Questions and Worked Solutions +
Given Data: \(L = 8\text{ m}\), \(W_1 = 64\text{ kN}\) at \(x_1 = 1\text{ m}\), \(W_2 = 48\text{ kN}\) at \(x_2 = 4\text{ m}\), \(E = 210\text{ GPa}\), \(I = 180 \times 10^6\text{ mm}^4\).
Conversion of Units

Since the loads and beam dimensions are expressed in kN and m, convert \(E\) and \(I\) into compatible units (kN and m).

\[\begin{aligned} E &= 210\text{ GPa}=210\times10^9\text{ N/m}^2\\ &= \frac{210\times10^9}{1000} =210\times10^6\text{ kN/m}^2\\ I &=180\times10^6\text{ mm}^4 =180\times10^6(10^{-3})^4\text{ m}^4\\ &=180\times10^{-6}=1.8\times10^{-4}\text{ m}^4 \end{aligned}\]
Flexural Rigidity (\(EI\))

Flexural rigidity is the product of Young's modulus and the second moment of area.

\[EI=(210\times10^6)(1.8\times10^{-4})=\mathbf{37{,}800\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}^2}\]
Support Reactions

Let \(R_A\) and \(R_B\) be the vertical reactions at the left and right supports respectively.

Step 1: Taking moments about support A

\[\begin{aligned} \sum M_A=0:\quad R_B(8)-64(1)-48(4)&=0\\ 8R_B&=64+192\\ 8R_B&=256\\ R_B&=\frac{256}{8}=\mathbf{32\text{ kN}} \end{aligned}\]

Step 2: Applying vertical force equilibrium

\[\begin{aligned} \sum V=0:\quad R_A+R_B-64-48&=0\\ R_A+32&=112\\ R_A&=\mathbf{80\text{ kN}} \end{aligned}\]
Macaulay's Deflection Equation

Taking a section at a distance \(x\) from the left support, the bending moment is written using Macaulay brackets. Here, \(\langle x-a\rangle^n=0\) when \(x<a\), and \((x-a)^n\) when \(x\geq a\).

\[M(x)=80x-64\langle x-1\rangle-48\langle x-4\rangle\]

Taking downward deflection as positive, the elastic curve equation is \(EI\,d^2y/dx^2=-M(x)\):

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} &=-80x+64\langle x-1\rangle+48\langle x-4\rangle\\ EI\frac{dy}{dx} &=-40x^2+32\langle x-1\rangle^2 +24\langle x-4\rangle^2+C_1\\ EI\,y &=-\frac{40}{3}x^3+\frac{32}{3}\langle x-1\rangle^3 +8\langle x-4\rangle^3+C_1x+C_2 \end{aligned}\]
Evaluation of Constants (\(C_1\) and \(C_2\))

Boundary condition 1: At the left support, \(x=0\), deflection \(y=0\).

\[EI(0)=-\frac{40}{3}(0)^3+0+0+C_1(0)+C_2=0\quad\therefore C_2=0\]

Boundary condition 2: At the right support, \(x=8\text{ m}\), deflection \(y=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} 0&=-\frac{40}{3}(8)^3+\frac{32}{3}(8-1)^3+8(8-4)^3+8C_1\\ 0&=-\frac{40}{3}(512)+\frac{32}{3}(343)+8(64)+8C_1\\ 0&=-\frac{20480}{3}+\frac{10976}{3}+512+8C_1\\ 8C_1&=-512+\frac{9504}{3}=2656\\ C_1&=\mathbf{332\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}^2} \end{aligned}\]

Therefore, the complete deflection equation is:

\[EI\,y=-\frac{40}{3}x^3+332x+\frac{32}{3}\langle x-1\rangle^3+8\langle x-4\rangle^3\]
Deflection Under Each Load

1. Deflection under the \(64\text{ kN}\) load (\(x=1\text{ m}\))

At \(x=1\), the brackets \(\langle1-1\rangle^3\) and \(\langle1-4\rangle^3\) are zero.

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_1&=-\frac{40}{3}(1)^3+332(1)\\ &=-13.333+332=318.667\\ y_1&=\frac{318.667}{37{,}800} =0.008430\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{8.43\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]

2. Deflection under the \(48\text{ kN}\) load (\(x=4\text{ m}\))

At \(x=4\), \(\langle4-1\rangle^3=(3)^3\), while \(\langle4-4\rangle^3=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_2&=-\frac{40}{3}(4)^3+332(4)+\frac{32}{3}(4-1)^3\\ &=-\frac{2560}{3}+1328+\frac{32}{3}(27)\\ &=-853.333+1328+288\\ &=762.667\\ y_2&=\frac{762.667}{37{,}800} =0.020176\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{20.18\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]
Maximum Deflection and Its Location

Maximum deflection occurs where the slope is zero, i.e. \(dy/dx=0\). The maximum point lies between the two loads, \(1<x<4\), so the term \(\langle x-4\rangle^2=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{dy}{dx}&=-40x^2+32(x-1)^2+332=0\\ -40x^2+32(x^2-2x+1)+332&=0\\ -8x^2-64x+364&=0\\ 2x^2+16x-91&=0\\ x&=\frac{-16\pm\sqrt{16^2-4(2)(-91)}}{2(2)}\\ &=\frac{-16\pm\sqrt{984}}{4} \end{aligned}\]

Taking the positive root that lies within the interval \(1<x<4\):

\[x=\mathbf{3.842\text{ m from the left support}}\]

Substitute \(x=3.842\text{ m}\) into the deflection equation. Since this point is before the second load, the term \(\langle x-4\rangle^3=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_{\max} &=-\frac{40}{3}(3.842)^3+332(3.842) +\frac{32}{3}(3.842-1)^3\\ &\approx764.24\\ y_{\max}&=\frac{764.24}{37{,}800} =0.020218\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{20.22\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]
Reactions: \(R_A=80\text{ kN}\), \(R_B=32\text{ kN}\). Deflection under 64 kN: \(8.43\text{ mm}\). Deflection under 48 kN: \(20.18\text{ mm}\). Maximum deflection: \(20.22\text{ mm}\) downward at \(3.842\text{ m}\) from the left support.
Given Data: \(L=6\text{ m}\), \(W_1=48\text{ kN}\) at \(x_1=1\text{ m}\), \(W_2=40\text{ kN}\) at \(x_2=3\text{ m}\), \(E=2\times10^5\text{ N/mm}^2\), \(I=85\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\).
Conversion of Units

Convert Young's modulus from N/mm² to kN/m² and the second moment of area from mm⁴ to m⁴.

\[\begin{aligned} E&=2\times10^5\text{ N/mm}^2 =2\times10^5\times10^6\text{ N/m}^2\\ &=2\times10^{11}\text{ N/m}^2 =2\times10^8\text{ kN/m}^2\\ I&=85\times10^6(10^{-3})^4 =85\times10^{-6}\text{ m}^4 \end{aligned}\]
Flexural Rigidity (\(EI\))
\[EI=(2\times10^8)(85\times10^{-6})=\mathbf{17{,}000\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}^2}\]
Support Reactions

Let \(R_A\) and \(R_B\) be the vertical reactions at the left and right supports respectively.

Step 1: Taking moments about A

\[\begin{aligned} \sum M_A=0:\quad R_B(6)-48(1)-40(3)&=0\\ 6R_B&=48+120=168\\ R_B&=\frac{168}{6}=\mathbf{28\text{ kN}} \end{aligned}\]

Step 2: Applying vertical force equilibrium

\[\begin{aligned} \sum V=0:\quad R_A+R_B-48-40&=0\\ R_A+28&=88\\ R_A&=\mathbf{60\text{ kN}} \end{aligned}\]
Deflection Equation (Macaulay's Method)

Taking a section at a distance \(x\) from the left support, the bending moment is:

\[M(x)=60x-48\langle x-1\rangle-40\langle x-3\rangle\]

Taking downward deflection as positive, use \(EI\,d^2y/dx^2=-M(x)\). Integrate once to obtain slope and a second time to obtain deflection.

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} &=-60x+48\langle x-1\rangle+40\langle x-3\rangle\\ EI\frac{dy}{dx} &=-30x^2+24\langle x-1\rangle^2 +20\langle x-3\rangle^2+C_1\\ EI\,y &=-10x^3+8\langle x-1\rangle^3 +\frac{20}{3}\langle x-3\rangle^3+C_1x+C_2 \end{aligned}\]
Evaluation of Constants (\(C_1\) and \(C_2\))

Boundary condition 1: At \(x=0\), \(y=0\).

\[0=0+0+0+0+C_2\quad\therefore C_2=0\]

Boundary condition 2: At \(x=6\text{ m}\), \(y=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} 0&=-10(6)^3+8(6-1)^3+\frac{20}{3}(6-3)^3+6C_1\\ 0&=-2160+8(125)+\frac{20}{3}(27)+6C_1\\ 0&=-2160+1000+180+6C_1\\ 6C_1&=980\\ C_1&=\mathbf{163.333\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}^2} \end{aligned}\]

Therefore, the complete deflection equation is:

\[EI\,y=-10x^3+163.333x+8\langle x-1\rangle^3+\frac{20}{3}\langle x-3\rangle^3\]
Deflections Under Each Load

1. Deflection under the \(48\text{ kN}\) load (\(x=1\text{ m}\))

At \(x=1\), both Macaulay bracket terms are zero.

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_1&=-10(1)^3+163.333(1)\\ &=153.333\\ y_1&=\frac{153.333}{17{,}000} =0.009020\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{9.02\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]

2. Deflection under the \(40\text{ kN}\) load (\(x=3\text{ m}\))

At \(x=3\), \(\langle3-1\rangle^3=2^3=8\), while \(\langle3-3\rangle^3=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_2&=-10(3)^3+163.333(3)+8(3-1)^3\\ &=-270+489.999+8(8)\\ &\approx-270+490+64=284\\ y_2&=\frac{284}{17{,}000} =0.016706\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{16.71\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]
Point and Value of Maximum Deflection

At the point of maximum deflection, the slope is zero. The required point lies between the two loads, \(1<x<3\), hence \(\langle x-3\rangle^2=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{dy}{dx}&=-30x^2+163.333+24(x-1)^2=0\\ -30x^2+163.333+24(x^2-2x+1)&=0\\ -6x^2-48x+187.333&=0\\ 18x^2+144x-562&=0 \end{aligned}\]

Solving the quadratic equation:

\[\begin{aligned} x&=\frac{-144\pm\sqrt{144^2-4(18)(-562)}}{2(18)}\\ &=\frac{-144\pm\sqrt{61200}}{36} \end{aligned}\]

Taking the positive root within the interval \(1<x<3\):

\[x=\mathbf{2.872\text{ m from the left support}}\]

Substitute this value into the deflection equation. Since \(x<3\), the bracket \(\langle x-3\rangle^3=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_{\max} &=-10(2.872)^3+163.333(2.872)+8(2.872-1)^3\\ &\approx284.68\\ y_{\max}&=\frac{284.68}{17{,}000} =0.016746\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{16.75\text{ mm (downward)}} \end{aligned}\]
Deflection under 48 kN: \(9.02\text{ mm}\). Deflection under 40 kN: \(16.71\text{ mm}\). Maximum deflection: \(16.75\text{ mm}\) downward at \(2.872\text{ m}\) from the left support.
Given Data: Cantilever beam of length \(L\), fixed at \(A\) (\(x=0\)), carrying a point load \(P\) vertically downward at the free end \(B\) (\(x=L\)). Young's modulus \(=E\), second moment of area \(=I\).
Bending Moment at a Section

Consider a section at a distance \(x\) from the fixed end. The portion of the beam to the right of the section carries the point load \(P\), acting at a distance \((L-x)\) from the section.

\[\begin{aligned} M(x)&=-P(L-x)\\ &=-PL+Px \end{aligned}\]

The negative sign indicates hogging bending moment for the chosen sign convention. From the elastic curve equation:

\[EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=-M(x)=PL-Px\]
Slope Equation — First Integration

Integrating the differential equation with respect to \(x\):

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}&=PL-Px\\ EI\int\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\,dx&=\int(PL-Px)\,dx\\ EI\frac{dy}{dx}&=PLx-\frac{Px^2}{2}+C_1 \end{aligned}\]

Boundary condition at the fixed end: At \(x=0\), the slope is zero, \(\left.dy/dx\right|_{x=0}=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI(0)&=PL(0)-\frac{P(0)^2}{2}+C_1\\ 0&=C_1\\ \therefore C_1&=0 \end{aligned}\]

Therefore, the slope at any section is:

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{dy}{dx}&=PLx-\frac{Px^2}{2}\\ \theta(x)=\frac{dy}{dx} &=\frac{P}{2EI}(2Lx-x^2) \end{aligned}\]

The positive slope represents clockwise rotation when downward deflection is taken as positive.

Deflection Equation — Second Integration

Integrating the slope equation once again with respect to \(x\):

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{dy}{dx}&=PLx-\frac{Px^2}{2}\\ EI\int dy&=\int\left(PLx-\frac{Px^2}{2}\right)dx\\ EI\,y&=\frac{PLx^2}{2}-\frac{Px^3}{6}+C_2 \end{aligned}\]

Boundary condition at the fixed end: At \(x=0\), vertical deflection is zero, \(y=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} EI(0)&=\frac{PL(0)^2}{2}-\frac{P(0)^3}{6}+C_2\\ 0&=C_2\\ \therefore C_2&=0 \end{aligned}\]

Hence, the deflection equation is:

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y&=\frac{PLx^2}{2}-\frac{Px^3}{6}\\ y(x)&=\frac{Px^2}{6EI}(3L-x) \end{aligned}\]

The positive value represents downward deflection.

\[\boxed{\delta(x)=\frac{Px^2}{6EI}(3L-x)}\]
Slope and Deflection at the Free End

At the free end, \(x=L\).

1. Slope at the free end

\[\begin{aligned} \theta_B&=\frac{P}{2EI}\left[2L(L)-L^2\right]\\ &=\frac{P}{2EI}(2L^2-L^2)\\ &=\frac{PL^2}{2EI} \end{aligned}\]

Maximum slope:

\[\boxed{\theta_{\max}=\frac{PL^2}{2EI}\text{ radians}}\]

2. Deflection at the free end

\[\begin{aligned} y_B&=\frac{PL(L)^2}{2EI}-\frac{P(L)^3}{6EI}\\ &=\frac{PL^3}{2EI}-\frac{PL^3}{6EI}\\ &=\frac{3PL^3-PL^3}{6EI}\\ &=\frac{2PL^3}{6EI}\\ &=\frac{PL^3}{3EI} \end{aligned}\]

Maximum downward deflection:

\[\boxed{y_{\max}=\frac{PL^3}{3EI}}\]
For a cantilever carrying a point load \(P\) at its free end: Maximum slope at the free end, \(\displaystyle\theta_{\max}=\frac{PL^2}{2EI}\) radians. Maximum deflection at the free end, \(\displaystyle y_{\max}=\frac{PL^3}{3EI}\) downward.
Given Data: \(L=3\text{ m}\), \(w=10\text{ kN/m}\), \(E=200\text{ GPa}\), \(I=80\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\). The UDL acts over the entire length of the cantilever.
Conversion of Units

Use kN and m throughout the calculation.

\[\begin{aligned} E&=200\text{ GPa}=200\times10^9\text{ N/m}^2\\ &=200\times10^6\text{ kN/m}^2\\ I&=80\times10^6(10^{-3})^4\\ &=80\times10^{-6}\text{ m}^4 \end{aligned}\]
Flexural Rigidity (\(EI\))
\[EI=(200\times10^6)(80\times10^{-6})=\mathbf{16{,}000\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}^2}\]
Resultant Load and Fixed-End Reactions

The uniformly distributed load over the full span can be replaced by an equivalent point load acting at the centre of the loaded length.

\[\begin{aligned} W&=wL=10(3)=30\text{ kN}\\ \text{Distance of resultant from fixed end}&=\frac{L}{2}=\frac{3}{2}=1.5\text{ m} \end{aligned}\]

Taking vertical force equilibrium:

\[\begin{aligned} \sum V=0:\quad R_A-W&=0\\ R_A&=\mathbf{30\text{ kN}} \end{aligned}\]

Taking moments about the fixed end:

\[\begin{aligned} \sum M_A=0:\quad M_A-W\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)&=0\\ M_A&=30(1.5)\\ M_A&=\mathbf{45\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}} \end{aligned}\]
Bending Moment at a Section

Consider a section at a distance \(x\) from the fixed end. The UDL acting on the portion to the right of the section has length \((L-x)\). Its resultant is \(w(L-x)\), acting at a distance \((L-x)/2\) from the section.

\[\begin{aligned} M(x)&=-w(L-x)\left(\frac{L-x}{2}\right)\\ &=-\frac{w}{2}(L-x)^2 \end{aligned}\]

Using the elastic curve equation:

\[EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=-M(x)=\frac{w}{2}(L-x)^2\]
Slope Equation — First Integration

Expanding the square and integrating with respect to \(x\):

\[\begin{aligned} EI\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} &=\frac{w}{2}(L^2-2Lx+x^2)\\ EI\frac{dy}{dx} &=-\frac{wL^2x}{2}+\frac{wLx^2}{2} +\frac{wx^3}{6}+C_1 \end{aligned}\]

Boundary condition at the fixed end: At \(x=0\), slope \(dy/dx=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} 0&=\frac{wL^2(0)}{2}-\frac{wL(0)^2}{2} +\frac{w(0)^3}{6}+C_1\\ \therefore C_1&=0 \end{aligned}\]

Thus, the slope equation becomes:

\[EI\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{wL^2x}{2}-\frac{wLx^2}{2}+\frac{wx^3}{6}\]
Deflection Equation — Second Integration

Integrating the slope equation with respect to \(x\):

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y&=\frac{wL^2x^2}{4}-\frac{wLx^3}{6} +\frac{wx^4}{24}+C_2 \end{aligned}\]

Boundary condition at the fixed end: At \(x=0\), deflection \(y=0\).

\[\begin{aligned} 0&=\frac{wL^2(0)^2}{4}-\frac{wL(0)^3}{6} +\frac{w(0)^4}{24}+C_2\\ \therefore C_2&=0 \end{aligned}\]

Therefore, the deflection equation is:

\[EI\,y=\frac{wL^2x^2}{4}-\frac{wLx^3}{6}+\frac{wx^4}{24}\]
Deflection at the Free End

At the free end, \(x=L=3\text{ m}\). Substitute this value into the deflection equation.

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,y_B&=\frac{wL^2(L)^2}{4} -\frac{wL(L)^3}{6}+\frac{w(L)^4}{24}\\ &=\frac{wL^4}{4}-\frac{wL^4}{6}+\frac{wL^4}{24}\\ &=\frac{wL^4}{24}(6-4+1)\\ &=\frac{wL^4}{8} \end{aligned}\]

The positive value indicates downward deflection. Hence:

\[\boxed{y_B=\frac{wL^4}{8EI}}\]
Numerical Calculation
\[\begin{aligned} y_B&=\frac{10(3)^4}{8(16{,}000)}\\ &=\frac{10(81)}{128{,}000}\\ &=\frac{810}{128{,}000}\\ &=0.006328125\text{ m}\\ &=\mathbf{6.33\text{ mm}} \end{aligned}\]
Deflection at the free end: \(y_B=\mathbf{6.33\text{ mm}}\) downward.
