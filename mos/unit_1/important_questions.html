Short Answer Questions – 2 Marks +
Temperature stress is the stress developed in a material when its free expansion or contraction due to a change in temperature is prevented.
\[\boxed{\sigma=E\alpha\Delta T}\]
where \(E\) is Young's modulus, \(\alpha\) is the coefficient of thermal expansion and \(\Delta T\) is the temperature change.
Strength: The ability of a material to withstand an applied load without failure.
Ductility: The ability of a material to undergo considerable plastic deformation before fracture.
Resilience: The capacity of a material to absorb energy within its elastic limit and release it when the load is removed.
Impact loading: A load applied suddenly to a body, producing a high rate of deformation and stress.
A composite bar is a bar made of two or more different materials rigidly connected together so that they act together under load.
Strain energy is the energy stored in a body due to deformation when an external load is applied.
\[\boxed{SE=\frac{1}{2}P\delta L}\]
Stress: Internal resisting force developed per unit cross-sectional area.
\[\boxed{\sigma=\frac{P}{A}}\]
SI unit of stress: Pa (N/m²).
Strain: Deformation per unit original dimension.
\[\boxed{\varepsilon=\frac{\delta L}{L}}\]
Strain is dimensionless and has no SI unit.
Proof resilience: The maximum strain energy stored in a body up to the elastic limit.
Modulus of resilience: Proof resilience per unit volume of the material.
\[\boxed{\text{Modulus of resilience}=\frac{\text{Proof resilience}}{\text{Volume}}}\]
Thermal stress: Stress developed due to prevented thermal expansion or contraction.
\[\boxed{\sigma=E\alpha\Delta T}\]
Thermal strain: Free strain produced in a material due to a change in temperature.
\[\boxed{\varepsilon_T=\alpha\Delta T}\]

Given:
\[ \begin{aligned} G&=0.5\times10^5\ \text{N/mm}^2\\ d&=10\ \text{mm},&L&=300\ \text{mm}\\ P&=10,000\ \text{N}\\ \delta d&=0.00015\ \text{mm},&\delta L&=0.015\ \text{mm} \end{aligned}\]
Poisson's Ratio:
Lateral Strain, \(\varepsilon_{\text{lat}}\)
\[\varepsilon_{\text{lat}}=\frac{\delta d}{d}=\frac{0.00015}{10}=0.000015\]
Longitudinal Strain, \(\varepsilon_{\text{long}}\)
\[\varepsilon_{\text{long}}=\frac{\delta L}{L}=\frac{0.015}{300}=0.00005\]
Poisson's Ratio, \(\mu\)
\[\mu=\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{lat}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{long}}}=\boxed{0.3}\]
Young's Modulus:
Young's Modulus, \(E\)
\[ \begin{aligned} E&=2G(1+\mu)\\ &=2(0.5\times10^5)(1+0.3)\\ &=\boxed{1.3\times10^5\ \text{N/mm}^2} \end{aligned}\]
Bulk Modulus:
Bulk Modulus of Elasticity, \(K\)
\[ \begin{aligned} K&=\frac{E}{3(1-2\mu)}\\ &=\frac{1.3\times10^5}{3(1-2(0.3))}\\ &=\frac{130000}{1.2}\\ &=\boxed{108333.33\ \text{N/mm}^2} \end{aligned}\]

Derivation:

Consider a gradually applied tensile load \(P\) producing an extension \(\delta L\). The load-extension graph is a straight line for an elastically loaded material.

Strain Energy, \(SE\)
\[SE=\text{Area under the load-extension graph}\]
\[SE=\frac{1}{2}P\delta L\]
Stress, \(\sigma\), and Load, \(P\)
\[\sigma=\frac{P}{A}\quad\Rightarrow\quad P=\sigma A\]
Extension, \(\delta L\)
\[ \begin{aligned} \delta L&=\frac{PL}{EA}\\ &=\frac{\sigma AL}{EA}\\ &=\frac{\sigma L}{E} \end{aligned}\]

Substituting \(P=\sigma A\) and \(\delta L=\frac{\sigma L}{E}\),

Strain Energy in terms of Stress
\[ \begin{aligned} SE&=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma A)\left(\frac{\sigma L}{E}\right)\\ &=\frac{\sigma^2AL}{2E} \end{aligned}\]

Since \(AL\) is the volume \(V\),

Strain Energy in terms of Stress, Modulus and Volume
\[\boxed{SE=\frac{\sigma^2V}{2E}}\]

Therefore, strain energy stored in a tensile member is:

Final Expression for Strain Energy
\[\boxed{SE=\frac{P^2L}{2AE}=\frac{\sigma^2}{2E}V}\]
Final expression: \(\boxed{SE=\dfrac{\sigma^2V}{2E}}\)
