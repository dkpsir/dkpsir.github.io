SHEAR STRESS DISTRIBUTION — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS
Shear stress distribution: questions and worked solutions Five questions
Shear Stress Distribution — Questions and Worked Solutions +

Notation: \(F\) = shear force; \(b\) = breadth; \(d\) = depth; \(y\) = distance of the layer from the neutral axis; \(I\) = second moment of area about the neutral axis; \(\tau\) = shear stress.

1. Basic shear stress equation
\[\tau=\frac{FQ}{Ib}=\frac{FA\bar y'}{Ib}\]

Here, \(Q=A\bar y'\) is the first moment of the area above the layer about the neutral axis.

2. Area above the considered layer
\[A=b\left(\frac d2-y\right)\]
3. Distance of the selected area's centroid from the neutral axis
\[\begin{aligned} \bar y'&=y+\frac12\left(\frac d2-y\right)\\ &=y+\frac d4-\frac y2\\ &=\frac12\left(y+\frac d2\right) \end{aligned}\]
4. Moment of inertia of the rectangular section
\[I=\frac{bd^3}{12}\]
5. Substitute into the shear stress equation
\[\begin{aligned} \tau&=\frac{F\left[b\left(\frac d2-y\right)\right]\left[\frac12\left(y+\frac d2\right)\right]}{Ib}\\ &=\frac{F}{2I}\left(\frac d2-y\right)\left(y+\frac d2\right)\\ &=\frac{F}{2I}\left(\frac{d^2}{4}-y^2\right) \end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned} \tau&=\frac{6F}{bd^3}\left(\frac{d^2}{4}-y^2\right)\\ &=\frac{3F}{2bd}\left(1-\frac{4y^2}{d^2}\right) \end{aligned}\]
Shear stress at any depth: \(\displaystyle\tau=\frac{3F}{2bd}\left(1-\frac{4y^2}{d^2}\right)\)
6. Maximum shear stress

At the neutral axis, \(y=0\).

\[\boxed{\tau_{\max}=\frac{3F}{2bd}=\frac32\frac{F}{A}}\]

At the top and bottom surfaces, \(y=\pm d/2\), so \(\tau=0\). The distribution is parabolic, with maximum stress at the neutral axis.

Given: \(D=200\text{ mm}\), \(t=10\text{ mm}\), \(F=25{,}000\text{ N}\).

\[\begin{aligned} R&=\frac D2=100\text{ mm}\\ r&=R-t=90\text{ mm}\\ d_i&=D-2t=180\text{ mm} \end{aligned}\]
1. Moment of inertia about the neutral axis
\[\begin{aligned} I&=\frac{\pi}{64}(D^4-d_i^4)\\ &=\frac{\pi}{64}(200^4-180^4)\\ &=\boxed{27.010\times10^6\text{ mm}^4} \end{aligned}\]
2. Maximum shear stress at the neutral axis

First moment of area of the upper half of the annulus:

\[\begin{aligned} Q_{\mathrm{NA}}&=\frac23(R^3-r^3)\\ &=\frac23(100^3-90^3)\\ &=180{,}666.67\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]

The material width at the neutral axis is \(b=2(R-r)=20\text{ mm}\).

\[\begin{aligned} \tau_{\max}&=\frac{FQ_{\mathrm{NA}}}{Ib}\\ &=\frac{25{,}000(180{,}666.67)}{(27.010\times10^6)(20)}\\ &\approx\boxed{8.36\text{ N/mm}^2} \end{aligned}\]
3. Shear stress at the inner edge

At the upper inner edge, \(y=r=90\text{ mm}\). The inner-circle segment above this tangent level has zero area, so \(Q\) is calculated from the outer-circle segment.

\[\begin{aligned} Q&=\frac23(R^2-y^2)^{3/2}\\ &=\frac23(100^2-90^2)^{3/2}\\ &=55{,}211.5\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]

Width of material at this level:

\[\begin{aligned} b&=2\sqrt{R^2-y^2}\\ &=2\sqrt{100^2-90^2}\\ &=87.18\text{ mm} \end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned} \tau_{\mathrm{inner}}&=\frac{FQ}{Ib}\\ &=\frac{25{,}000(55{,}211.5)}{(27.010\times10^6)(87.18)}\\ &\approx\boxed{0.586\text{ N/mm}^2} \end{aligned}\]
Maximum shear stress: \(\tau_{\max}\approx8.36\text{ MPa}\). Inner-edge shear stress: \(\tau_{\mathrm{inner}}\approx0.586\text{ MPa}\).

Section dimensions: flange \(150\times50\text{ mm}\); web \(50\times150\text{ mm}\); total depth \(200\text{ mm}\); \(F=50{,}000\text{ N}\).

The span is not needed in the calculation because the shear force is already given.

Centroid: 125 mm from bottom. \(I=53.125\times10^6\text{ mm}^4\). Maximum shear stress: \(7.353\text{ MPa}\).

Given: \(F=200{,}000\text{ N}\); each flange \(200\times20\text{ mm}\); web \(20\times360\text{ mm}\); total depth \(400\text{ mm}\).

1. Centroid

The section is symmetrical; therefore, the neutral axis is at mid-depth:

\[\bar y=\frac{400}{2}=\boxed{200\text{ mm from bottom}}\]
2. Moment of inertia
\[\begin{aligned} I&=2\left[\frac{200(20)^3}{12}+(200\times20)(190)^2\right]+\frac{20(360)^3}{12}\\ &=2[133{,}333.33+4000(190)^2]+77.76\times10^6\\ &=\boxed{366.8267\times10^6\text{ mm}^4} \end{aligned}\]
3. Shear stress at the flange–web junction
\[\begin{aligned} Q_f&=(200\times20)(200-10)\\ &=4000(190)=760{,}000\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]

Flange side, \(b=200\text{ mm}\):

\[\tau_f=\frac{200{,}000(760{,}000)}{(366.8267\times10^6)(200)}=\boxed{2.073\text{ N/mm}^2}\]

Web side, \(b=20\text{ mm}\):

\[\tau_w=\frac{200{,}000(760{,}000)}{(366.8267\times10^6)(20)}=\boxed{20.726\text{ N/mm}^2}\]
4. Maximum shear stress at the neutral axis
\[\begin{aligned} Q_{\mathrm{NA}}&=(4000)(190)+(20\times180)(90)\\ &=760{,}000+324{,}000\\ &=1{,}084{,}000\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]
\[\tau_{\max}=\frac{200{,}000(1{,}084{,}000)}{(366.8267\times10^6)(20)}=\boxed{29.55\text{ N/mm}^2}\]
Maximum shear stress: \(\boxed{29.55\text{ MPa}}\), at the neutral axis in the web.

Given: \(D=600\text{ mm}\), flange width \(=200\text{ mm}\), flange thickness \(=30\text{ mm}\), web thickness \(=20\text{ mm}\), \(F=500{,}000\text{ N}\).

\[\begin{aligned} h_w&=600-2(30)=540\text{ mm}\\ \text{Neutral axis}&=300\text{ mm from top or bottom} \end{aligned}\]
1. Moment of inertia
\[\begin{aligned} I&=2\left[\frac{200(30)^3}{12}+(200\times30)(285)^2\right]+\frac{20(540)^3}{12}\\ &=2[450{,}000+6000(285)^2]+262.44\times10^6\\ &=\boxed{1.23804\times10^9\text{ mm}^4} \end{aligned}\]
2. Shear stress at the flange–web junction
\[\begin{aligned} Q_f&=(200\times30)(300-15)\\ &=6000(285)=1{,}710{,}000\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]

Flange side, \(b=200\text{ mm}\):

\[\tau_f=\frac{500{,}000(1{,}710{,}000)}{(1.23804\times10^9)(200)}=\boxed{3.454\text{ N/mm}^2}\]

Web side, \(b=20\text{ mm}\):

\[\tau_w=\frac{500{,}000(1{,}710{,}000)}{(1.23804\times10^9)(20)}=\boxed{34.53\text{ N/mm}^2}\]
3. Maximum shear stress at the neutral axis
\[\begin{aligned} Q_{\mathrm{NA}}&=(200\times30)(285)+(20\times270)(135)\\ &=1{,}710{,}000+729{,}000\\ &=2{,}439{,}000\text{ mm}^3 \end{aligned}\]
\[\tau_{\max}=\frac{500{,}000(2{,}439{,}000)}{(1.23804\times10^9)(20)}=\boxed{49.25\text{ N/mm}^2}\]
4. Shear force carried by the web

The shear force carried by the web is the integral of shear stress over the web area. Let \(y\) be measured from the neutral axis, with \(-270\le y\le270\text{ mm}\).

\[\begin{aligned} Q(y)&=6000(285)+20(270-y)\frac{270+y}{2}\\ &=2{,}439{,}000-10y^2\\ \tau(y)&=\frac{FQ(y)}{I\,t_w} \end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned} V_{\mathrm{web}}&=\int_{-270}^{270}\tau(y)t_w\,dy\\ &=\frac{F}{I}\int_{-270}^{270}(2{,}439{,}000-10y^2)\,dy\\ &=\frac{500{,}000}{1.23804\times10^9} \left[2{,}439{,}000(540)-10\frac{2(270)^3}{3}\right]\\ &=\boxed{478.92\text{ kN (approximately)}} \end{aligned}\]
Maximum shear stress: \(\tau_{\max}\approx49.25\text{ MPa}\). Shear force carried by the web: \(V_{\mathrm{web}}\approx478.92\text{ kN}\), about \(95.78\%\) of the total shear force.
