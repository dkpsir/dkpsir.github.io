Question 1: Hollow Circular Cross-Section

Problem: A simply supported beam of length 4 m carries a point load of 16 kN at a distance of 3 m from the left support. The cross section of the beam is a hollow circle with outer diameter 120 mm and inside diameter 80 mm. Determine the maximum tensile and compressive bending stress at the point load.

1. Maximum Bending Moment

$$M_{\max} = \frac{W \cdot a \cdot b}{L} = \frac{16 \times 3 \times 1}{4} = 12\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$M_{\max} = 12 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$$

2. Moment of Inertia

$$I = \frac{\pi}{64}(D^4 - d^4) = \frac{\pi}{64}(120^4 - 80^4)$$

$$I = 8.168 \times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$$

3. Bending Stress Calculation

$$y = \frac{D}{2} = 60\text{ mm}$$

$$\sigma_{\max} = \frac{M_{\max} \cdot y}{I} = \frac{12 \times 10^6 \times 60}{8.168 \times 10^6}$$

$$\sigma_{\max} = 88.15\text{ N/mm}^2\text{ (MPa)}$$

Question 3: T-Section Load Capacity (\(w\)) Determination

Problem: A simply supported beam of T cross-sectional area has a span of 10 m. It is carrying a uniformly distributed load of 'w' N/m along the span. Determine the value of w, if the allowable bending stress is limited to 150 MPa in tension and 220 MPa in compression.

Section Properties & Centroid Table (Bottom Reference)

Part No. Area \(A\) (\(\text{mm}^2\)) \(y\) from bottom (\(\text{mm}\)) \(A y\) (\(\text{mm}^3\)) \(h = |y - \bar{y}|\) (\(\text{mm}\)) \(I_0 + A h^2\) (\(\text{mm}^4\))
1 (Web) \(50 \times 150 = 7500\) \(75\) \(562,500\) \(50\) $$32.8125 \times 10^6$$
2 (Flange) \(150 \times 50 = 7500\) \(175\) \(1,312,500\) \(50\) $$20.3125 \times 10^6$$
Total \(A = 15,000\) — \(\sum Ay = 1,875,000\) — \(I = 53.125 \times 10^6\text{ mm}^4\)

1. Centroid & Distances

$$\bar{y} = \frac{1,875,000}{15,000} = 125\text{ mm}$$

$$y_{\text{bottom}} = 125\text{ mm},\quad y_{\text{top}} = 75\text{ mm}$$

2. Moment Capacity Evaluation

$$M_t = \frac{I \cdot \sigma_t}{y_{\text{bottom}}} = \frac{53.125 \times 10^6 \times 150}{125} = 63.75\text{ kNm}$$

$$M_c = \frac{I \cdot \sigma_c}{y_{\text{top}}} = \frac{53.125 \times 10^6 \times 220}{75} = 155.83\text{ kNm}$$

Governing \(M_{\text{allow}} = 63.75\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}\)

3. Load Carrying Capacity

$$M_{\max} = \frac{w L^2}{8}$$

$$63.75 \times 10^6 = \frac{w(10000)^2}{8}$$

$$w = 5100\text{ N/m} = 5.1\text{ kN/m}$$

Question 4: Unsymmetrical I-Section Load Capacity (\(w\))

Problem: A beam of I-section is simply supported over a span of 4 m. Determine the load that the beam can carry, if the allowable bending stress in beam is 30.82 N/mm². (Bottom flange 100x20 mm, Web 30x100 mm, Top flange 60x20 mm).

Section Properties & Centroid Table (Bottom Reference)

Part No. Area \(A\) (\(\text{mm}^2\)) \(y\) from bottom (\(\text{mm}\)) \(A y\) (\(\text{mm}^3\)) \(h = |y - \bar{y}|\) (\(\text{mm}\)) \(I_0 + A h^2\) (\(\text{mm}^4\))
1 (Bottom Flange) \(100 \times 20 = 2000\) \(10\) \(20,000\) \(52.26\) $$5.528 \times 10^6$$
2 (Web) \(30 \times 100 = 3000\) \(70\) \(210,000\) \(7.74\) $$2.680 \times 10^6$$
3 (Top Flange) \(60 \times 20 = 1200\) \(130\) \(156,000\) \(67.74\) $$5.547 \times 10^6$$
Total \(A = 6200\) — \(\sum Ay = 386,000\) — \(I = 13.755 \times 10^6\text{ mm}^4\)

1. Centroid Location

$$\bar{y} = \frac{386,000}{6200} = 62.26\text{ mm}$$

$$y_{\text{top}} = 140 - 62.26 = 77.74\text{ mm}$$

2. Allowable Bending Moment

Top fiber is farther from N.A., so compression governs:

$$M_{\text{allow}} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{allow}} \cdot I}{y_{\text{top}}} = \frac{30.82 \times 13.755 \times 10^6}{77.74}$$

$$M_{\text{allow}} = 5.453 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$$

3. Load Carrying Capacity

$$w = \frac{8 M_{\text{allow}}}{L^2} = \frac{8 \times 5.453 \times 10^6}{(4000)^2}$$

$$w = 2.726\text{ N/mm} = 2.726\text{ kN/m}$$

Question 5: Assumptions of Bending Equation Derivation

Question 5: State the primary assumptions made in the derivation of the simple / pure bending equation $$\frac{M}{I} = \frac{\sigma}{y} = \frac{E}{R}$$.
  1. Initially Straight Beam: The beam is initially straight, has a constant cross-sectional area throughout its length, and possesses a longitudinal plane of symmetry along which external loads are applied.
  2. Homogeneous and Isotropic Material: The beam material is perfectly homogeneous (uniform composition throughout) and isotropic (possesses identical elastic properties in all directions).
  3. Proportionality Within Elastic Limit: The material obeys Hooke's Law strictly within its elastic limit, meaning the modulus of elasticity (\(E\)) remains constant in both tension and compression.
  4. Large Radius of Curvature: The radius of curvature (\(R\)) of the beam after bending is extremely large compared to its cross-sectional dimensions (height/depth).
